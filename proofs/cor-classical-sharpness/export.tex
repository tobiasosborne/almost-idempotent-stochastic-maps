\begin{proofbox}{Classical square-root sharpness: for every 0 < lambda < 1...}
\step{1} Classical square-root sharpness: for every 0 < lambda < 1/2, choose positive unital maps A\_lambda:l-infinity(2)->l-infinity(4) and M\_lambda:l-infinity(4)->l-infinity(2) supplied by lem-prh-sharpness, and put eta\_lambda=2*lambda\textasciicircum{}2 and Q\_lambda=A\_lambda M\_lambda; then Q\_lambda is row-stochastic, ||Q\_lambda\textasciicircum{}2-Q\_lambda|$|\_\{infinity-\rangle$infinity\} <= eta\_lambda, and every stochastic idempotent F on l-infinity(4) satisfies ||Q\_lambda-F|$|\_\{infinity-\rangle$infinity\} >= lambda=sqrt(eta\_lambda/2). Consequently, for every C>0, eta\_0>0, and beta>1/2 there exist 0<eta<min\{eta\_0,1/4\} and a row-stochastic Q on l-infinity(4) with ||Q\textasciicircum{}2-Q|$|\_\{infinity-\rangle$infinity\} <= eta such that every stochastic idempotent E satisfies ||Q-E|$|\_\{infinity-\rangle$infinity\} > C*eta\textasciicircum{}beta; equivalently, no uniform exponent beta>1/2 can replace 1/2 in op-classical. \steptag{validated} \\[6pt]
\step{1.1} Fix an arbitrary real 0 < lambda < 1/2. By the external lem-prh-sharpness, choose positive unital maps A\_lambda:l-infinity(2)->l-infinity(4) and M\_lambda:l-infinity(4)->l-infinity(2) such that epsilon\_lambda=||M\_lambda A\_lambda-I\_2|$|\_\{infinity-\rangle$infinity\}=2*lambda\textasciicircum{}2 and such that its stated distance conclusion holds. Define eta\_lambda:=2*lambda\textasciicircum{}2 and Q\_lambda:=A\_lambda M\_lambda. Then eta\_lambda=epsilon\_lambda, and since lambda>0, lambda=sqrt(eta\_lambda/2). \steptag{validated} \\[4pt]
\step{1.2} For the data chosen in 1.1, Q\_lambda is row-stochastic and ||Q\_lambda\textasciicircum{}2-Q\_lambda|$|\_\{infinity-\rangle$infinity\}<=eta\_lambda. Indeed, def-positive-approximate-retract says that the matrices of the positive unital maps A\_lambda and M\_lambda have probability-vector rows. Hence their product Q\_lambda has nonnegative entries and row sums one, so it is row-stochastic by def-stochastic. A probability-row matrix has infinity-to-infinity operator norm one (its maximum absolute row sum is one), so ||A\_lambda||=||M\_lambda||=1. Matrix associativity and the identity maps on the relevant spaces give Q\_lambda\textasciicircum{}2-Q\_lambda=A\_lambda M\_lambda A\_lambda M\_lambda-A\_lambda I\_2 M\_lambda=A\_lambda(M\_lambda A\_lambda-I\_2)M\_lambda. Submultiplicativity, 1.1, and eta\_lambda=2*lambda\textasciicircum{}2 therefore give ||Q\_lambda\textasciicircum{}2-Q\_lambda||<=||A\_lambda|| ||M\_lambda A\_lambda-I\_2|| ||M\_lambda||=eta\_lambda. This is precisely the weak defect bound in def-almost-idempotent whenever eta\_lambda<1/4. \steptag{validated} \\[4pt]
\step{1.3} For the data chosen in 1.1, every stochastic idempotent F on l-infinity(4) satisfies ||Q\_lambda-F|$|\_\{infinity-\rangle$infinity\}>=lambda=sqrt(eta\_lambda/2). This is exactly the distance conclusion of the external lem-prh-sharpness, namely ||A\_lambda M\_lambda-F||>=lambda=sqrt(epsilon\_lambda/2), after the definitional substitutions Q\_lambda=A\_lambda M\_lambda and eta\_lambda=epsilon\_lambda from 1.1. \steptag{validated} \\[4pt]
\step{1.4} Let arbitrary C>0, eta\_0>0, and beta>1/2 be given. All three numbers 1/(2*sqrt(2)), sqrt(eta\_0/2), and (C*2\textasciicircum{}beta)\textasciicircum{}(-1/(2*beta-1)) are positive, so choose 0<lambda strictly below their minimum, and set eta:=eta\_lambda=2*lambda\textasciicircum{}2 and Q:=Q\_lambda as in 1.1. The first cutoff gives lambda<1/2 and eta<1/4; the second gives eta<eta\_0; hence 0<eta<min\{eta\_0,1/4\}. By 1.2, Q is row-stochastic and ||Q\textasciicircum{}2-Q||<=eta. Since 2*beta-1>0, the third strict cutoff implies C*2\textasciicircum{}beta*lambda\textasciicircum{}(2*beta)<lambda. By 1.3, every stochastic idempotent E satisfies ||Q-E||>=lambda, while C*eta\textasciicircum{}beta=C*(2*lambda\textasciicircum{}2)\textasciicircum{}beta=C*2\textasciicircum{}beta*lambda\textasciicircum{}(2*beta)<lambda; thus every such E satisfies ||Q-E||>C*eta\textasciicircum{}beta. Finally, a uniform replacement exponent beta>1/2 would mean that some fixed C>0 and eta\_0>0 work in every dimension for every row-stochastic Q of defect at most eta<=eta\_0, producing a stochastic idempotent E with ||Q-E||<=C*eta\textasciicircum{}beta. Applying the just-constructed dimension-four witness to those proposed C and eta\_0 gives the literal contradiction that every E has ||Q-E||>C*eta\textasciicircum{}beta. Therefore no uniform beta>1/2 can replace 1/2. \steptag{validated} \\[4pt]
\end{proofbox}
