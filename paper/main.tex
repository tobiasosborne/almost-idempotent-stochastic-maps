\begin{abstract}
We prove a dimension-free stability theorem for almost-idempotent stochastic
matrices, with the sharp square-root modulus.  The entry point is an exact,
constant-one seam from a stochastic map on $\linf^n$ to a UCP map on $M_n$.
After an audited repair of the diagonal step in Kitaev's factorization, the
resulting finite-dimensional $C^*$-factorization descends to a positive
approximate retract; an elementary hardening argument then returns to the
stochastic category without sacrificing positivity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

We regard a row-stochastic matrix $Q$ as a positive unital map on the real
space $\linf^n$.  Exact stochastic idempotents are the finite Markov analogue
of conditional expectations onto partitions: on each recurrent class they
collapse onto one probability distribution, while transient rows retain
convex weights among the recurrent classes.  Their normal form is classical;
see \cite{HognasMukherjea,BaakeSumner}.  At defect zero there is of course
nothing to prove: $Q^2=Q$ allows $E=Q$.  The point below is that the
neighbourhood in which this remains quantitatively true does not shrink with
the number of states or recurrent classes.

\begin{theorem}[Stochastic stability]\label{thm:main}
There are universal constants $\eta_0,C>0$, independent of $n$, such that if
$Q:\linf^n\to\linf^n$ is row-stochastic and
\[
  \norm{Q^2-Q}_{\infty\to\infty}\leq \eta\leq\eta_0,
\]
then there is a row-stochastic idempotent $E$ satisfying
\[
  \norm{Q-E}_{\infty\to\infty}\leq C\sqrt\eta.
\]
For the explicit universal $K\geq1$ and $\eta_K>0$ produced by the
factorization ledger in Theorem~\ref{thm:factor}, one may take
\[
  \eta_0=\eta_K,\qquad C=K+4\sqrt{2K}.
\]
No uniform estimate with $\eta^\beta$ in place of $\sqrt\eta$ can hold for
any $\beta>\tfrac12$.
\end{theorem}\footnotemark
\footnotetext{The full argument --- every component lemma, the
factorization ledger, the assembly, and the top-level statement itself ---
has been machine-adversarially validated in this project's registry
(independent prover and hostile verifier per proof node).  This is an
adversarial machine protocol, not a proof-assistant formalization: no
Lean/mathlib proof exists, and the sharpness example is at present
verified only by human audit.}

Kitaev's approximate-algebra theorem \cite{Kitaev} is the large structural
input, which we use rather than reproduce.  The new work is to cross into its
completely bounded setting with no loss, repair the diagonal used in the
factorization, audit all constants uniformly over matrix amplifications and
block data, and then descend from a finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebra to an
actual stochastic idempotent.

\section{The diagonal seam}

Let $D:M_n\to\C^n$ be diagonal extraction and
$J:\C^n\to M_n$ diagonal inclusion, so that $DJ=I_{\C^n}$.  If
$Q_{\C}:\C^n\to\C^n$ denotes the canonical complexification of $Q$, put
\[
  \Phi:=JQ_{\C}D:M_n\longrightarrow M_n.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact seam]\label{lem:seam}
$\Phi$ is UCP and
\[
  \norm{\Phi^2-\Phi}_{\cb}
  =\norm{Q^2-Q}_{\infty\to\infty}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The map $Q_{\C}$ is positive and unital on the commutative $C^*$-algebra
$\C^n$, hence completely positive; $D$ and $J$ are UCP as well.  Thus so is
$\Phi$.  With $L=Q_{\C}^2-Q_{\C}$, the identity $DJ=I$ gives
$\Phi^2-\Phi=JLD$.  Complete contractivity of $D$ and complete isometry of
$J$ give the upper bound.  Conversely, testing at each matrix level on
diagonal inputs $J_rX$ and using $D_rJ_r=I$ gives
$\norm{J_rL_rD_r}\geq\norm{L_r}$.  Finally, for a map between finite
commutative $C^*$-algebras the completely bounded norm is its ordinary norm,
and the sign/phase test gives
$\norm{L}=\max_i\sum_j|L_{ij}|$.  This is precisely the
$\linf\!\to\!\linf$ norm of $Q^2-Q$.
\end{proof}

The equality in Lemma~\ref{lem:seam}, including its lower-bound direction,
is what makes the noncommutative machinery useful here: the original defect
$\eta$ enters the matrix-algebra picture without a dimensional or numerical
conversion factor.

\section{The repaired factorization ledger}

For a UCP $\Phi$ with $\norm{\Phi^2-\Phi}_{\cb}\leq\eta<\tfrac14$, Kitaev's
functional calculus uses
\[
 \widetilde\Phi=\frac12\left[I+(2\Phi-I)
       \bigl(I-4(\Phi-\Phi^2)\bigr)^{-1/2}\right]
\]
and the product $X\star Y=\widetilde\Phi(XY)$ on
$\mathcal A=\operatorname{im}\widetilde\Phi$.  Set
\[
 r=\frac32\bigl((1-4\eta)^{-1/2}-1\bigr),\qquad
 \varepsilon_{\rm AI}(\eta)
 =\max\{r,\,20\eta+2((1+r)^5-1),\,3r-r^2\}.
\]
There are universal $\eta_A>0$ and
$C_A=20+\frac{211}{8}C_\theta$, where $C_\theta=12(\sqrt2-1)$, such that
whenever $0\leq\eta\leq\eta_A$ the inherited operator-space norms,
involution, and unit make $\mathcal A$ an extended
$\varepsilon_{\rm AI}(\eta)$-$C^*$-algebra and
\[
 \varepsilon_{\rm AI}(\eta)\leq C_A\eta.
\]
The audited extension interface supplies universal $C_E<\infty$ and
$\varepsilon_E>0$: whenever $\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_E$, a
finite-dimensional extended $\varepsilon$-$C^*$-algebra is the image of a
finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebra under an extended
$C_E\varepsilon$-isomorphism.  These constants are independent of dimension,
amplification level, number of simple blocks, and their sizes.

One step in the printed factorization needs a genuine repair.  The proposed
direct-sum diagonal is not central, already for $\C\oplus\C$.  For
$\mathcal B=\bigoplus_{a=1}^mM_{d_a}$ we instead use a finite phase-balanced
diagonal
\[
  \mathfrak d_{\mathcal B}=\sum_tq_tW_t^*\otimes W_t,\qquad
  q_t\geq0,\quad \sum_tq_t=1,\quad
  z\mathfrak d_{\mathcal B}=\mathfrak d_{\mathcal B}z,\quad
  \pi(\mathfrak d_{\mathcal B})=1_{\mathcal B},\quad
  \norm{\mathfrak d_{\mathcal B}}_\pi=1.
\]
Independent signs on the simple blocks cancel all cross-block tensors.  The
same exact centrality makes the repaired map
\[
 \Delta'(x)=\sum_tq_t\,\Phi\!\left(
   \widetilde\Delta(xW_t^*)\widetilde\Delta(W_t)\right)
\]
completely positive whenever $\widetilde\Delta$ preserves the involution;
no false exact-multiplicativity premise is used.  This phase balancing and
the resulting CP-ization are the repair contributed here.

The remaining estimates are bookkeeping, but the bookkeeping is essential.
The scalar ledger first fixes one universal witness package from
$(\eta_A,C_A,C_E,\varepsilon_E)$.  Its finite sum, product, maximum,
reciprocal, and minimum formulas define coefficients
$C_\Delta,C_{\Delta'},C_2,C_\Upsilon$ and positive radii
$\rho_2,\rho_{\Delta\Upsilon},\rho_{\rm mult},
\rho_{\Upsilon\Delta}$.  In particular, its final three formulas are
\begin{align}
 K&=\max\bigl\{1,\ C_\theta+C_\Delta+2C_\Upsilon,\
 C_\Upsilon+2(C_2+C_\theta+C_\Delta),\
 C_\Upsilon+2C_\Delta\bigr\},\label{eq:K}\\
 \rho_{\rm fac}&=\min\{\rho_2,\rho_{\Delta\Upsilon},
 \rho_{\rm mult},\rho_{\Upsilon\Delta}\},\qquad
 \eta_K=\min\{\rho_{\rm fac},(24K)^{-1},1\}.
 \label{eq:etaK}
\end{align}
Every quantity on the right is a fixed scalar from the preceding repaired
factorization steps; none counts dimensions, amplifications, or blocks.

\begin{theorem}[Dimension-free factorization ledger]\label{thm:factor}
Let $K$ and $\eta_K$ be given by \eqref{eq:K}--\eqref{eq:etaK}.  For every
$n\geq1$, every row-stochastic $Q:\linf^n\to\linf^n$, and every
$0\leq\eta\leq\eta_K$ with
$\norm{Q^2-Q}_{\infty\to\infty}\leq\eta$, form $\Phi=JQ_{\C}D$ as above.
There are a finite-dimensional unital $C^*$-algebra $\mathcal B$ and UCP maps
\[
 \Delta:\mathcal B\to M_n,\qquad \Upsilon:M_n\to\mathcal B
\]
such that
\begin{align*}
 \norm{\Delta\Upsilon-\Phi}_{\cb}&\leq K\eta,&
 \norm{\Upsilon\Delta-I_{\mathcal B}}_{\cb}&\leq K\eta,\\
 \norm{\Upsilon_r(\Delta_rX\,\Delta_rY)-XY}_{M_r(\mathcal B)}
 &\leq K\eta\norm{X}_{M_r(\mathcal B)}\norm{Y}_{M_r(\mathcal B)}
 &&(r\geq1,\ X,Y\in M_r(\mathcal B)).
\end{align*}
Moreover, the same $Q$ admits a stochastic idempotent $E$ with
\[
 \norm{Q-E}_{\infty\to\infty}
 \leq (K+4\sqrt{2K})\sqrt\eta.
\]
\end{theorem}

The factorization assertions are Kitaev's construction, with the corrected
almost-idempotent interface and diagonal above, assembled through the
dimension-free ledger.  The last assertion is the stochastic descent, to
which we now turn.

\section{Descent to the stochastic category}

Apply the factorization theorem.  Its approximate multiplicativity and its
closeness to the diagonal-range map $\Phi$ force $\mathcal B$ to be
commutative at the threshold \eqref{eq:etaK}; a noncommutative simple block
would contain a norm-two commutator, contradicting the corresponding
factorization estimate.  Hence $\mathcal B\cong\C^k$.  After fixing a unital
$*$-isomorphism $\iota:\C^k\to\mathcal B$, restrict the diagonal parts to
the real self-adjoint subspaces:
\[
 A=(D\Delta\iota)|_{\R^k}:\linf^k\to\linf^n,\qquad
 M=(\iota^{-1}\Upsilon J)|_{\R^n}:\linf^n\to\linf^k.
\]
These maps are positive and unital.  The level-one factorization estimates
give, in the $\linf\!\to\!\linf$ norm,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F2}
 \norm{Q-AM}\leq K\eta,\qquad
 \norm{QA-A}\leq2K\eta,\qquad
 \norm{Ax}_\infty\geq(1-3K\eta)\norm{x}_\infty.
\end{equation}
This is the decisive categorical crossing: completely positive maps on
matrix algebras have become honest stochastic matrices, with no signed
intermediate projection.

The retract defect follows directly.  For $x\in\linf^k$,
\[
 A(MA-I_k)x=(AM-Q)Ax+(QA-A)x.
\]
Since $A$ is unital and positive, $\norm A=1$; combining this identity with
\eqref{eq:F2} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F3}
 \norm{MA-I_k}_{\infty\to\infty}
 \leq\frac{3K\eta}{1-3K\eta}.
\end{equation}

It remains to harden this positive approximate retract.  We record the short
argument because it is both elementary and the unique source of the square
root.  Write the rows of $A$ as probability vectors $a_i$ and those of $M$
as probability measures $\mu_s$.  If
$\norm{MA-I_k}_{\infty\to\infty}\leq\varepsilon<\tfrac12$, set
$\lambda=\sqrt{\varepsilon/2}$ and
\[
 C_s=\{i:a_{is}>1-\lambda\}.
\]
The $C_s$ are disjoint, and the $s$-th row estimate gives
\[
 \sum_i\mu_s(i)(1-a_{is})\leq\frac\varepsilon2,\qquad
 \beta_s:=\mu_s(C_s^c)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2\lambda}.
\]
Condition $\mu_s$ on $C_s$ to obtain the rows of a stochastic map $N$, and
replace each encoder row $a_i$, $i\in C_s$, by the vertex $e_s$ to obtain
$\widehat A$.  Then $N\widehat A=I_k$ exactly, so
$E=\widehat A N$ is stochastic and $E^2=E$.  Conditioning and hardening cost
respectively at most $\varepsilon/\lambda$ and $2\lambda$, whence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PRH}
 \norm{AM-E}_{\infty\to\infty}\leq2\sqrt{2\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
For $\varepsilon=0$, simply take $E=AM$.

Now $\eta\leq(24K)^{-1}$ makes the right side of \eqref{eq:F3} at most
$4K\eta<\tfrac12$.  Equations \eqref{eq:F2} and \eqref{eq:PRH}, together
with $\eta\leq1$, yield
\[
 \norm{Q-E}_{\infty\to\infty}
 \leq K\eta+4\sqrt{2K\eta}
 \leq (K+4\sqrt{2K})\sqrt\eta.
\]
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main} except for sharpness.

\section{Sharpness and remarks}

For $0<s<1$, consider the explicit $3\times3$ family
\[
 P_s=I-u_sv_s^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 v_s=(1,-1+s,-s),\qquad
 u_s=(1-s+s^2,-s,0)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
It is a signed affine retraction with maximal row negative mass
$\delta_s=s^2$.  If $\mathcal I_{\rm stoch}$ denotes the set of stochastic
idempotents, then
\[
 \operatorname{dist}_{\infty\to\infty}(P_s,\mathcal I_{\rm stoch})
 :=\inf_{F\in\mathcal I_{\rm stoch}}\norm{P_s-F}_{\infty\to\infty}
 =2s-2s^2+2s^3
 =2\sqrt{\delta_s}+O(\delta_s).
\]
Normalize the positive part of each row to obtain a row-stochastic $Q_s$.
The signed--stochastic bridge gives
\[
 \norm{P_s-Q_s}_{\infty\to\infty}\leq2s^2,\qquad
 \norm{Q_s^2-Q_s}_{\infty\to\infty}\leq6s^2+4s^4.
\]
Hence every $F\in\mathcal I_{\rm stoch}$ satisfies
\[
 \norm{Q_s-F}_{\infty\to\infty}\geq2s-4s^2+2s^3=2s(1-s)^2,
\]
which is $\Omega(s)$ as $s\downarrow0$ and excludes every exponent
$\beta>\tfrac12$.

All losses before \eqref{eq:PRH} are linear in $\eta$.  The square root is
born precisely when almost-disjoint stochastic cores are selected and their
measures are conditioned.  The sharp family therefore identifies not merely
the optimal exponent, but also the step at which an argument preserving
positivity must spend it.  Related dimension-free square-root repair
phenomena for fixed points of classical and quantum channels appear in
\cite{SalzmannBerghDatta}.
