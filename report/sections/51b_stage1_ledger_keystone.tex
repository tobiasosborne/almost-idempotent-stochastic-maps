\section{The Stage-1 keystone: the group-law transport and the single-tuple constant ledger}\label{sec:stage1-ledger-keystone}

This section closes the parameterized Stage-1 polar layer. The first row is the last of the
seven transports of \S\ref{sec:stage1-polar-transports}--\S\ref{sec:stage1-polar-transports-ii};
the second is the \emph{keystone} that consumes all seven at one and the same witness tuple;
the third is an algebra-independent finite-poset row that consumes none of them. The witness
datum $W$ and its fields are those of Definition~\ref{def:stage1-polar-witness-data}; all
ambient vocabulary is that of Definition~\ref{def:approximate-unitary-space} and

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{lem-stage1-approximate-group-laws-transport}]\label{lem:stage1-approximate-group-laws-transport}
There exist $C^{0}_{\mathrm{grp}},C^{0}_{\mathrm{pol}}\ge1$ and
$\kappa^{0}_{\mathrm{pol}}\in(0,\tfrac12]$ such that for every witness datum $W$ with
$C_{\mathrm{grp}}\ge C^{0}_{\mathrm{grp}}$, $C_{\mathrm{pol}}\ge C^{0}_{\mathrm{pol}}$ and
$0<\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}\le\kappa^{0}_{\mathrm{pol}}$, for every finite-dimensional
exact-unit $\ecs_r$-$C^*$-algebra and every $\delta>0$ with
$C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\ecs_r+\delta)\le\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}$ and
$C_{\mathrm{grp}}\ecs_r<\delta-C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\ecs_r\delta+\delta^{2})$: writing
$(u_\delta,h_\delta)$ for the unique inverse of $\Pi_\delta$ (the explicit binder of
Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-polar-retraction-transport}), the formulas
$\mu(U,V)=u_\delta(U\bdot V)$ and $\sigma(U)=u_\delta(U\dg)$ define $C^{1}$ maps on all of
$\Unit\times\Unit$ and $\Unit$, and for all $U,V,Z\in\Unit$ one has
$\mu(J,U)=\mu(U,J)=U$, $\sigma(J)=J$, and the five estimates
$\nrm{\mu(U,V)-U\bdot V}$, $\nrm{\sigma(U)-U\dg}$,
$\nrm{\mu(\mu(U,V),Z)-\mu(U,\mu(V,Z))}$, $\nrm{\mu(\sigma(U),U)-J}$,
$\nrm{\mu(U,\sigma(U))-J}\;\le\;C_{\mathrm{grp}}\ecs_r$.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
\emph{Witnesses first.} Fix $(G_d,P_d,k_d)$ from
\texttt{lem-stage1-explicit-group-domain-membership}, $(G_c,P_c,k_c)$ from
\texttt{lem-stage1-explicit-group-closeness} and $(P_r,k_r)$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-polar-retraction}, and set
$C^{0}_{\mathrm{grp}}=\max\{G_d,8G_c,8\}$, $C^{0}_{\mathrm{pol}}=\max\{P_d,P_c,P_r\}$ and
$\kappa^{0}_{\mathrm{pol}}=\min\{k_d,k_c,k_r,\tfrac1{16}\}$ --- \emph{before} the receiving
tuple $W$ is quantified. Writing $e=\ecs_r$ and $y=e\delta+\delta^{2}\ge0$, the receiving
guards give $P_i(e+\delta)\le\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}\le k_i$ for $i\in\{d,c,r\}$ and
$G_ie<\delta-P_iy$ for $i\in\{d,c\}$; also $0\le e<\tfrac1{16}$.

\emph{Synchronization.} All three providers display \emph{literally} the same
$\Pi_\delta(U,H)=U\bdot H$ on the same domain $\Unit\times B^{\Herm}_{\delta}(J)$ with the
same image $S_\delta$, so ordinary uniqueness of the inverse of one bijection identifies
their three inverse pairs with each other and with the $(u_\delta,h_\delta)$ named in the
root. Coherence naturality is neither needed nor imported. The domain bridge then puts
$U\bdot V$ and $U\dg$ in $S_\delta$ for all $U,V\in\Unit$, so $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are
globally defined with values in $\Unit$; $u_\delta$ is $C^{1}$ on $S_\delta$ and both input
maps are $C^{1}$ as real maps in finite dimension, so $\mu,\sigma$ are $C^{1}$.

\emph{Uniform estimates.} For $T\in\Unit$, $T\dg\bdot T=J$ and a right inverse $R$ exist; the
$C^*$-lower bound with $\nrm{J}=1$ gives $\nrm{T}\le a:=(1-e)^{-1/2}$, so for $e<\tfrac1{16}$
multiplying a perturbation by a unitary costs at most $(1+e)a<2$ and an associator of three
unitaries at most $ea^{3}\le2e$. Exact unitality with the associator bound gives
$\nrm{R-T\dg}\le e\nrm{T}^{2}\nrm{R}$, hence $\nrm{R}\le\nrm{T}(1-e)/(1-2e)$ and
$\nrm{T\bdot T\dg-J}\le e(1+e)/((1-e)(1-2e))\le2e$. These apply to $\mu(A,B)$ and
$\sigma(A)$ too, since $u_\delta$ takes values in $\Unit$.

\emph{The laws.} Exact unitality gives $J\bdot U=U\bdot J=U$ and $J\dg=J$, while the
synchronized retraction identity gives $u_\delta(U)=U$ for $U\in\Unit$; hence
$\mu(J,U)=\mu(U,J)=U$ and $\sigma(J)=J$. The closeness bridge gives both
$\nrm{\mu(U,V)-U\bdot V}\le G_ce$ and $\nrm{\sigma(U)-U\dg}\le G_ce$. For associativity the
two outer retraction errors contribute $G_ce$ each, replacing $\mu(U,V)$ by $U\bdot V$ and
$\mu(V,Z)$ by $V\bdot Z$ inside one multiplication costs at most $2G_ce$ on each side, and
the algebra associator at most $2e$: total $(6G_c+2)e\le C_{\mathrm{grp}}e$, since
$C_{\mathrm{grp}}\ge8G_c$ and $C_{\mathrm{grp}}\ge8$. For the inverse laws,
$\nrm{\mu(\sigma(U),U)-\sigma(U)\bdot U}\le G_ce$, $\nrm{\sigma(U)\bdot U-U\dg\bdot U}\le2G_ce$
and $U\dg\bdot U=J$ give $\nrm{\mu(\sigma(U),U)-J}\le3G_ce$; on the other side the extra
term $\nrm{U\bdot U\dg-J}\le2e$ gives $\nrm{\mu(U,\sigma(U))-J}\le(3G_c+2)e$. Both are at
most $C_{\mathrm{grp}}\ecs_r$.

new analysis, only the fact that seven separately-quantified thresholds admit a common

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{lem-finite-polyhedron-maximal-simplex-placement}]\label{lem:finite-polyhedron-maximal-simplex-placement}
Every point of a finite polyhedron lies in a maximal simplex of its defining finite
simplicial complex; therefore every point of every finite fixed set does.

\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
Let $K$ be the defining finite simplicial complex and let $x\in|K|$. By the definition of
the geometric realization, $x$ lies in some simplex $\rho$ of $K$. The simplices of $K$
containing $\rho$ form a nonempty finite partially ordered set under inclusion, hence
have a maximal element $\tau$; were $\tau$ properly contained in a simplex of $K$, that
simplex would also contain $\rho$, contradicting maximality in this set. So $\tau$ is a
maximal simplex of $K$ and $x\in\tau$. Now let $F$ be any finite fixed set inside that
polyhedron and apply the pointwise conclusion separately to each $x\in F$: for every $x\in F$
there is a maximal simplex $\tau_x$ with $x\in\tau_x$, the simplex being permitted to depend
on $x$. That dependence is precisely what the amended second clause asserts. The argument is
algebra-independent and uses only finite-poset and simplicial facts, which are common
knowledge and carry no definition shard.
