\section{The complete four-corner merge}\label{sec:extcb-four-corner-merge}

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{lem-extcb-four-corner-merge}]\label{lem:extcb-four-corner-merge}
There are universal $C_{\mathrm{merge}}<\infty$ and $a_{\mathrm{merge}}>0$ such that four
fixed bijective level-one corner maps satisfying \texttt{def-four-corner-merging-datum}
with common defect $\rho$ and $\rho+\ecs\le a_{\mathrm{merge}}$ combine into one extended
$C_{\mathrm{merge}}(\rho+\ecs)$-isomorphism.
\emph{Transcription note.} The smallness hypothesis constrains the \emph{total} defect
$\rho+\ecs$, not $\rho$ alone; that is a verdict-driven amendment of the transcribed
contract, and the record of why it cannot be dropped is reproduced below. ``Extended
isomorphism'' is \texttt{def-extended-delta-inclusion}: every amplification is a
$\delta$-homomorphism with two-sided $(1\pm\delta)$ norm bounds, and the level-one map is
bijective.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textbf{Registry contract (verbatim).}
\contractquote{Complete four-corner merge: there are universal C_merge < infinity and a_merge > 0 such that four fixed bijective level-one corner maps satisfying def-four-corner-merging-datum with common defect rho and rho+epsilon <= a_merge combine into one extended C_merge*(rho+epsilon)-isomorphism.}

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
Write $\pi_j=\ampel{n}{\Pi_j}$, $X_{jk}=\pi_jX\pi_k$, and let $\mu_n,\Gamma_n,\alpha_n$
and $\gamma_n=\alpha_n\Gamma_n\mu_n$ be the assembly maps of
\S\ref{sec:extcb-four-corner-norm}. Set $\comb=\rho+\ecs$.

\emph{One map, not a family.} Exact source complementarity gives
$\pi_j\pi_k=\delta_{jk}\pi_j$, $\pi_1+\pi_2=I$ and $X=\sum_{jk}X_{jk}$; since the four
$\gamma_{jk,n}$ are the amplifications of the same four level-one maps,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fcm-single}
  \gamma_n=\ampl{n}\gamma_1,\qquad
  \gamma_1(X)=\sum_{j,k}\gamma_{jk}(\Pi_jX\Pi_k).
\end{equation}
This is the structural point of the lemma: the conclusion is about \emph{one} linear map
and its amplifications, so nothing may be chosen level by level. Two clauses follow at
once. Adjoints of source corners transpose indices, $(\pi_jX\pi_k)\dg=\pi_kX\dg\pi_j$, and
the datum's involution clause matches them, so $\gamma_n(X\dg)=\gamma_n(X)\dg$
\emph{exactly}. The source unit has only diagonal blocks, so
$\gamma_n(I)-I$ splits into the two diagonal-unit errors and
$\ampl{n}(P_1+P_2-I)$, whose norm equals its level-one value by the direct-sum norm
axiom; hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fcm-unit}
  \nrm{\gamma_n(I)-I}\;\le\;3\rho ,\qquad\text{uniformly in }n .
\end{equation}

\emph{Corner separation and the multiplicative defect.} As in
\S\ref{sec:extcb-four-corner-norm}, compression commutes with amplification, so all
matrix-level corner estimates are available with level-one constants; and from
$p_kp_l=p_k(s-I)+(p_kI-p_k)-(p_k^{2}-p_k)$ with $s=p_1+p_2$ one gets
$\nrm{p_kp_l}\le5\comb$ for $k\ne l$ once $\comb\le\tfrac14$. Hence a product of corner
elements is compressed-product-close when the middle indices match
($\nrm{UV-U\cpr V}\le c_{\mathrm{prod}}\comb\nrm{U}\nrm{V}$) and is itself small when they
do not ($\nrm{UV}\le c_{\mathrm{cross}}\comb\nrm{U}\nrm{V}$, by three associator moves
that expose $p_kp_l$). Expanding $\gamma_n(XY)$ and $\gamma_n(X)\gamma_n(Y)$ over the
blocks gives sixteen terms --- eight with matching middle index, compared through the
datum's compressed-product clause, and eight without, which are small --- so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fcm-mult}
  \nrm{\gamma_n(XY)-\gamma_n(X)\gamma_n(Y)}\;\le\;D_{\mathrm{mult}}\,\comb\,\nrm{X}\nrm{Y},
  \qquad D_{\mathrm{mult}}=8\bigl[1+2c_{\mathrm{prod}}+2c_{\mathrm{cross}}\bigr],
\end{equation}
independently of $n$ and of all dimensions.

\emph{Norm control.} The datum and the assembly \eqref{eq:fcm-single} are exactly the
hypotheses of \texttt{lem-extcb-four-corner-norm}
(\S\ref{sec:extcb-four-corner-norm}), which supplies the two-sided bound
$(1-K_{\mathrm{norm}}\comb)\nrm{X}\le\nrm{\gamma_n(X)}\le(1+K_{\mathrm{norm}}\comb)\nrm{X}$
at every level simultaneously.

\emph{Coverage, and bijectivity at level one.} Injectivity of $\gamma_1$ is immediate from
the lower bound once $K_{\mathrm{norm}}\comb\le\tfrac12$. Surjectivity is the delicate
half, and it is obtained without assuming that the target corner sum is direct: with
$\beta(Y)=(\Co{P_j}{P_k}Y)_{jk}$, each compression is $O(\comb)$-close to
$Y\mapsto P_j(YP_k)$, so $\alpha_1\beta$ is $O(\comb)$-close to $Y\mapsto s(Ys)$ with
$s=P_1+P_2$ --- an identity of bilinearity alone, no associativity used --- and
$\nrm{s-I}\le\rho$ together with the product and approximate-unit axioms gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fcm-cov}
  \nrm{\alpha_1\beta-I}\;\le\;C_{\mathrm{cov}}\comb .
\end{equation}
Choosing $\comb$ with $C_{\mathrm{cov}}\comb<1$ makes $\alpha_1\beta$ invertible by its
Neumann series, so $Y=\alpha_1\bigl(\beta((\alpha_1\beta)^{-1}Y)\bigr)$ for every $Y$ and
$\alpha_1$ is onto. Now $\mu_1$ is a bijection by exact source complementarity, $\Gamma_1$
is a bijection because all four fixed level-one maps are bijective by hypothesis, and
$\alpha_1$ is onto; therefore $\gamma_1=\alpha_1\Gamma_1\mu_1$ is onto, hence bijective,
and every $\ampl{n}\gamma_1$ is algebraically bijective with inverse
$\ampl{n}\gamma_1^{-1}$.

\emph{Conclusion.} Let $a_{\mathrm{merge}}$ be the minimum of the thresholds above ---
$\comb_{\mathrm{corner}}$, $\comb_{\mathrm{norm}}$, $\comb_{\mathrm{cov}}$,
$1/(2K_{\mathrm{norm}})$ and the universal compression radius --- and put
$C_{\mathrm{merge}}=\max\{3,D_{\mathrm{mult}},K_{\mathrm{norm}}\}$. Then
\eqref{eq:fcm-unit} and \eqref{eq:fcm-mult} make every amplification a
$C_{\mathrm{merge}}\comb$-homomorphism, the norm lemma supplies the two-sided
$(1\pm C_{\mathrm{merge}}\comb)$ bounds, and level-one bijectivity supplies the
isomorphism clause; \texttt{def-extended-delta-inclusion} then returns exactly the
asserted extended $C_{\mathrm{merge}}(\rho+\ecs)$-isomorphism.

\paragraph{Why the hypothesis is on the total defect.} The transcribed contract bounded
only $\rho$, leaving $\ecs$ free. That version is \emph{false}, and the tree records an
exact counterexample rather than a repair. Take $\mathcal A=\mathbb C^{3}$ with
coordinatewise involution, the multiplication
$(a,b,c)(a',b',c')=(aa',bb',0)$ and designated unit $I=(1,1,0)$: at every matrix level
this is an extended $1$-$C^*$-algebra, the lower $C^*$ inequality being vacuous at
$\ecs=1$. The elements $P_1=(1,0,0)$ and $P_2=(0,1,0)$ are exact projections with
$P_1+P_2=I$, so $\rho=0$; the registered $\theta$ formula gives $\Cos{P_j}=E_j$ and
$\Co{P_j}{P_k}=0$ for $j\ne k$, so the four target corners are
$\mathbb C P_1,0,0,\mathbb C P_2$. With $\mathcal B=\mathbb C^{2}$ and its exact
structure, the two diagonal identities and the two zero maps form a datum with common
defect $\rho=0$ whose four maps are bijective complete isometries; yet the assembled map
is $\gamma(a,b)=(a,b,0)$, which is not surjective, and no linear bijection
$\mathbb C^{2}\to\mathbb C^{3}$ exists at all. So $\rho$ may be arbitrarily small while
the conclusion fails, and only a ceiling on $\rho+\ecs$ repairs it. The prover amended the
root accordingly and the fresh verifiers validated the amended statement; the registry
contract carries that amended root verbatim, and downstream consumption supplies a small
ambient $\ecs$, so the conditional form is the consumable one.
