\section{The smooth polar inverse, smooth operations, and the polar arithmetic}\label{sec:stage1-smooth-upgrades}

The three rows of this section close the \texttt{af}-validated part of the Stage-1 polar
block begun in \S\ref{sec:stage1-polar-charts}. The first two are smoothness upgrades in
the style of Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-smooth-unitary-atlas}: they replace no map, no point and
no first derivative, but promote the $C^{1}$ regularity of the polar inverse and of the
approximate-group operations to $C^{\infty}$ for the embedded smooth structure. The third
is pure scalar arithmetic: it packages, once and for all, the guard inequalities that every
analytic row of the block demands, so that downstream consumers can instantiate the whole
front from a single smallness assumption on $\ecs_X$.

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{lem-stage1-smooth-polar-inverse}]\label{lem:stage1-smooth-polar-inverse}
For every finite-dimensional exact-unit $\ecs_r$-$C^*$-algebra and every $\delta>0$: if
Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-smooth-unitary-atlas} gives the smooth embedded atlas and
$\Pi_\delta:\Unit\times B^{\Herm}_{\delta}(J)\to S_\delta$ is the bijective $C^{1}$ local
diffeomorphism of Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-polar-retraction} onto an open set, then the same
ambient-bilinear $\Pi_\delta$ is a smooth diffeomorphism and its same set-theoretic inverse
$(u_\delta,h_\delta)$ is smooth; no point or first derivative is changed.
\emph{Transcription note.} Like the atlas row, this is qualitative and conditional: both
hypotheses are supplied by the two named lemmas, no new threshold is introduced, and the
conclusion re-asserts nothing about \emph{which} $\delta$ realises them.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textbf{Registry contract (verbatim).}
\contractquote{Smooth polar-inverse upgrade: for every finite-dimensional exact-unit epsilon_r-C*-algebra and every delta > 0, if lem-stage1-smooth-unitary-atlas gives the smooth embedded atlas and Pi_delta: calU x B^{calH}_delta(J) -> S_delta is the bijective C^1 local diffeomorphism of lem-stage1-polar-retraction onto an open set, then the same ambient-bilinear Pi_delta is a smooth diffeomorphism and its same set-theoretic inverse (u_delta, h_delta) is smooth; no point or first derivative is changed.}

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
\emph{The two sides are smooth manifolds.} Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-smooth-unitary-atlas}
equips $\Unit$ with the smooth embedded graph atlas whose charts and first derivatives are
those of the $C^{1}$ atlas; $\Herm$ is a real linear subspace of the finite-dimensional
ambient space (Definition~\ref{def:approximate-unitary-space}), so $B^{\Herm}_{\delta}(J)$
is an open subset of a finite-dimensional real vector space, and
$\Unit\times B^{\Herm}_{\delta}(J)$ carries the product smooth structure; $S_\delta$ is
open in the ambient space by Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-polar-retraction} and carries its
open-subset structure.

\emph{$\Pi_\delta$ is smooth.} By Definition~\ref{def:epsilon-cstar-algebra} the product
$\bdot$ is bilinear, so in real coordinates each component of $(X,H)\mapsto X\bdot H$ is a
quadratic polynomial, hence $C^{\infty}$. In a product chart built from a graph chart of
the atlas and the identity chart on the ball, the coordinate representative of
$\Pi_\delta$ is this polynomial map composed with the smooth ambient parametrisation of
the embedded chart, hence smooth; such charts cover the domain and the values lie in
$S_\delta$, so $\Pi_\delta$ is smooth.

\emph{Everywhere-invertible derivative.} At any point $p$ the $C^{1}$
local-diffeomorphism assertion of Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-polar-retraction} provides a local
$C^{1}$ inverse $G$; the chain rule applied to $G\circ\Pi_\delta=\mathrm{id}$ and
$\Pi_\delta\circ G=\mathrm{id}$ shows $D\Pi_\delta(p)$ is a linear isomorphism.

\emph{Smooth local inverses, glued.} Fix $p$, put $q=\Pi_\delta(p)$, and choose smooth
charts $\alpha$ about $p$ and $\beta$ about $q$. The coordinate map
$F=\beta\circ\Pi_\delta\circ\alpha^{-1}$ is smooth with invertible derivative at
$\alpha(p)$; Theorem~C.34 (Inverse Function Theorem) of J.\,M. Lee, \emph{Introduction to
Smooth Manifolds}, 2nd ed., Springer GTM 218, 2012 --- registered in the workspace as the
byte-matched external \texttt{GT-lee-2ed-thm-C.34} against the pinned local source at the
recorded locus --- makes $F$ a smooth diffeomorphism between neighbourhoods, so
$\Pi_\delta$ has a smooth local inverse near $q$. By the bijectivity assertion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-polar-retraction} the global set-theoretic inverse exists; on each
such target neighbourhood it coincides with the unique smooth local inverse there, and
these neighbourhoods cover $S_\delta$, so the global inverse is smooth because smoothness
is local --- exactly the gluing recorded in Corollary~C.36(b) of the same source, the
byte-matched external \texttt{GT-lee-2ed-cor-C.36}. A bijection has only one set-theoretic
inverse, so this smooth inverse \emph{is} the pair $(u_\delta,h_\delta)$ already supplied.

\emph{Nothing is changed.} The atlas upgrade is identity-on-data through first order, the
map is literally the same $\Pi_\delta(U,H)=U\bdot H$ on the same domain and codomain, and
the inverse is the same set map; the derivative of a $C^{1}$ function in unchanged
first-order charts is unique, so proving these same functions $C^{\infty}$ changes no
point value and no first derivative.

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
\emph{The circle preserves $\Unit$.} If $U\dg\bdot U=J$ and $U\bdot R=J$, then for
$c\in U(1)$ conjugate-linearity of $\dagger$ and bilinearity give
$(cU)\dg\bdot(cU)=\bar cc\,J=J$ and $(cU)\bdot(\bar cR)=J$, so $cU\in\Unit$ with the
explicit right inverse $\bar cR$. This local derivation is repeated wherever membership is
needed below, deliberately never citing a sibling node.

\emph{The scalar action is smooth.} Since $J\in B^{\Herm}_{\delta}(J)$ and
$\Pi_\delta(U,J)=U\bdot J=U$ by exact unitality, both $U$ and $cU$ lie in the open set
$S_\delta$; the ambient map $(c,U)\mapsto cU$, the restriction of complex scalar
multiplication, is smooth into $S_\delta$, and composing with the smooth
$u_\delta:S_\delta\to\Unit$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-smooth-polar-inverse} gives a smooth
$\Unit$-valued map. The scalar-naturality clause \eqref{eq:s1cn-natural} of
Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-polar-coherence-naturality} gives $u_\delta(cU)=c\,u_\delta(U)=cU$,
so this smooth composite \emph{is} the action itself.

\emph{$\mu$ and $\sigma$ are smooth.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-approximate-group-laws} the
inputs $U\bdot V$ and $U\dg$ lie in $S_\delta$ for all $U,V\in\Unit$ and the displayed
formulas are the same globally defined $C^{1}$ maps. The ambient product is bilinear and
$\dagger$ is conjugate-linear, hence both restrict to smooth $S_\delta$-valued maps on the
embedded domain; composing with the smooth $u_\delta$ makes $\mu$ and $\sigma$ smooth into
$\Unit$.

\emph{Covariance.} By the local membership derivation, $cU,dV\in\Unit$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-approximate-group-laws} puts $X=U\bdot V$ and
$Y=(cU)\bdot(dV)$ in $S_\delta$; bilinearity gives $Y=(cd)X$ with $cd\in U(1)$, and
\eqref{eq:s1cn-natural} gives $u_\delta(Y)=cd\,u_\delta(X)$, the first identity of
\eqref{eq:s1so-covariance}. Likewise $(cU)\dg=\bar cU\dg$ with $\bar c\in U(1)$, both
$U\dg$ and $(cU)\dg$ lie in $S_\delta$, and \eqref{eq:s1cn-natural} with scalar $\bar c$
gives $\sigma(cU)=\bar c\,\sigma(U)$.

\emph{Nothing is changed.} The atlas and the polar inverse retain their point values and
first derivatives by Lemmas~\ref{lem:stage1-smooth-unitary-atlas} and
\ref{lem:stage1-smooth-polar-inverse}; the ambient scalar, product and adjoint maps are
untouched; and $\mu,\sigma$ keep the identical formulas of
Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-approximate-group-laws}. Uniqueness of derivatives through the
unchanged component derivatives leaves every first derivative the pre-existing one.

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{lem-stage1-polar-scalar-arithmetic}]\label{lem:stage1-polar-scalar-arithmetic}
For every $C_{\mathrm{rect}},C_{\mathrm{ch}},C_{\mathrm{pol}},C_{\mathrm{grp}},
C_{\mathrm{path}},C_{\mathrm{der}}\ge1$, every
$e_{\mathrm{rect}}\in(0,1/C_{\mathrm{rect}}]$ and every
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}},\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}},\kappa_{\mathrm{der}}\in(0,\tfrac12]$, set
\begin{align}
  \delta_*&=\min\Bigl\{\tfrac14,\;\tfrac{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}}{4C_{\mathrm{ch}}},\;
    \tfrac{\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}}{4C_{\mathrm{pol}}}\Bigr\},
  &
  \varepsilon_*^{r}&=\min\Bigl\{\tfrac14,\;\tfrac{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}}{4C_{\mathrm{ch}}},\;
    \tfrac{\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}}{4C_{\mathrm{pol}}},\;
    \tfrac{\kappa_{\mathrm{der}}}{8C_{\mathrm{der}}},\;
    \tfrac1{C_{\mathrm{grp}}},\;
    \tfrac{\delta_*}{12C_{\mathrm{path}}C_{\mathrm{grp}}}\Bigr\},
  \label{eq:s1sa-scales}\\
  e_{S1}&=\min\Bigl\{e_{\mathrm{rect}},\;\tfrac{\varepsilon_*^{r}}{C_{\mathrm{rect}}}\Bigr\},
  &
  r_{\mathrm{iso}}&=\min\Bigl\{\tfrac{\delta_*}4,\;\tfrac{\kappa_{\mathrm{der}}}{8C_{\mathrm{der}}}\Bigr\},
  \nonumber
\end{align}
and, given $0\le\ecs_X\le e_{S1}$, put $\ecs_r=C_{\mathrm{rect}}\ecs_X$,
$q=C_{\mathrm{grp}}\ecs_r$,
$r_-=\delta_*-C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\ecs_r\delta_*+\delta_*^{2})$ and
$\eta=C_{\mathrm{path}}(q+\ecs_rq+q^{2})$. Then every such $\ecs_X$ satisfies
\begin{gather}
  C_{\mathrm{ch}}(\ecs_r+\delta_*)\le\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}},\qquad
  C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\ecs_r+\delta_*)\le\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}},\qquad
  q<r_-,\qquad
  C_{\mathrm{path}}q\le\tfrac14,
  \label{eq:s1sa-guards1}\\
  \eta<r_-,\qquad
  C_{\mathrm{der}}(\ecs_r+r_{\mathrm{iso}})\le\kappa_{\mathrm{der}},\qquad
  (1+\ecs_r)\bigl(1+C_{\mathrm{ch}}(\ecs_r+\delta_*)\bigr)r_{\mathrm{iso}}+q<2\delta_*;
  \label{eq:s1sa-guards2}
\end{gather}
moreover $r_-\ge3\delta_*/4$, $\eta\le\delta_*/4$ and
$C_{\mathrm{der}}(r_{\mathrm{iso}}+\ecs_r)\le\kappa_{\mathrm{der}}/4<1$.
\emph{Transcription note.} The row is universal over every already-selected coefficient
and margin tuple and carries \emph{no} analytic content: its ten inputs and four derived
scales are exactly the fields of the witness tuple of
Definition~\ref{def:stage1-polar-witness-data}, and the seven displayed guards are the
hypotheses of the analytic rows of
\S\S\ref{sec:stage1-polar-charts}--\ref{sec:stage1-smooth-polar} at the scales
$\delta=\delta_*$ and $r=r_{\mathrm{iso}}$.
\end{lemma}

\contractquote{Universal Stage-1 polar arithmetic: for every C_rect, C_ch, C_pol, C_grp, C_path, C_der >= 1, e_rect in (0, 1/C_rect], and kappa_ch, kappa_pol, kappa_der in (0, 1/2], setting delta_* = min{1/4, kappa_ch/(4*C_ch), kappa_pol/(4*C_pol)}, epsilon_*^r = min{1/4, kappa_ch/(4*C_ch), kappa_pol/(4*C_pol), kappa_der/(8*C_der), 1/C_grp, delta_*/(12*C_path*C_grp)}, e_S1 = min{e_rect, epsilon_*^r/C_rect}, r_iso = min{delta_*/4, kappa_der/(8*C_der)}, epsilon_r = C_rect*epsilon_X, q = C_grp*epsilon_r, r_- = delta_* - C_pol*(epsilon_r*delta_* + delta_*^2), and eta = C_path*(q + epsilon_r*q + q^2), every 0 <= epsilon_X <= e_S1 satisfies C_ch*(epsilon_r + delta_*) <= kappa_ch, C_pol*(epsilon_r + delta_*) <= kappa_pol, q < r_-, C_path*q <= 1/4, eta < r_-, C_der*(epsilon_r + r_iso) <= kappa_der, and (1 + epsilon_r)*(1 + C_ch*(epsilon_r + delta_*))*r_iso + q < 2*delta_*; moreover r_- >= 3*delta_*/4, eta <= delta_*/4, and C_der*(r_iso + epsilon_r) <= kappa_der/4 < 1.}

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
\emph{Channel and polar guards.} From $e_{S1}\le\varepsilon_*^{r}/C_{\mathrm{rect}}$ and
$C_{\mathrm{rect}}\ge1$, $0\le\ecs_r\le\varepsilon_*^{r}$. Since both
$\varepsilon_*^{r}$ and $\delta_*$ are at most $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}/(4C_{\mathrm{ch}})$
and $\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}/(4C_{\mathrm{pol}})$, the first two guards hold with value at
most $\kappa/2$; and since
$r_-=\delta_*\bigl[1-C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\ecs_r+\delta_*)\bigr]$ with
$C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\ecs_r+\delta_*)\le\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}/2\le\tfrac14$, one gets
$r_-\ge3\delta_*/4$.

\emph{Path guards.} $\ecs_r\le\delta_*/(12C_{\mathrm{path}}C_{\mathrm{grp}})$ gives
$0\le q\le\delta_*/(12C_{\mathrm{path}})\le\delta_*/12$, hence
$C_{\mathrm{path}}q\le\delta_*/12\le\tfrac1{48}<\tfrac14$ (using $\delta_*\le\tfrac14$)
and $q<3\delta_*/4\le r_-$. From $\ecs_r\le\tfrac14$ and $q\le\tfrac1{48}$,
$1+\ecs_r+q\le\tfrac{61}{48}<3$, so
$\eta=C_{\mathrm{path}}q\,(1+\ecs_r+q)\le\delta_*/4<3\delta_*/4\le r_-$.

\emph{Derivative guards.} $\ecs_r\le\kappa_{\mathrm{der}}/(8C_{\mathrm{der}})$ and
$r_{\mathrm{iso}}\le\kappa_{\mathrm{der}}/(8C_{\mathrm{der}})$ give
$C_{\mathrm{der}}(\ecs_r+r_{\mathrm{iso}})\le\kappa_{\mathrm{der}}/4$; and
$0<\kappa_{\mathrm{der}}\le\tfrac12$ makes
$\kappa_{\mathrm{der}}/4\le\kappa_{\mathrm{der}}$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{der}}/4\le\tfrac18<1$.

\emph{The product guard.} The minima give $\ecs_r\le\tfrac14$,
$C_{\mathrm{ch}}(\ecs_r+\delta_*)\le\tfrac14$, $r_{\mathrm{iso}}\le\delta_*/4$ and
$q\le\delta_*/12$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s1sa-product}
  (1+\ecs_r)\bigl(1+C_{\mathrm{ch}}(\ecs_r+\delta_*)\bigr)r_{\mathrm{iso}}+q
  \;\le\;\tfrac54\cdot\tfrac54\cdot\tfrac{\delta_*}4+\tfrac{\delta_*}{12}
  \;=\;\tfrac{91}{192}\,\delta_*\;<\;2\delta_* ,
\end{equation}
strictly, because $\delta_*>0$.
