\section{Positive-retract hardening}\label{sec:prh}

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{lem-prh}]\label{lem:prh}
Let $k,n\ge1$ and let
\[
  A:\linfd{k}\longrightarrow\linfd{n},
  \qquad
  M:\linfd{n}\longrightarrow\linfd{k}
\]
be positive unital maps --- equivalently, maps whose matrices have probability-vector
rows. If
\[
  \opnorm{MA-I_k}\;\le\;\varepsilon,\qquad 0\le\varepsilon<\tfrac12,
\]
then there is a stochastic idempotent $E:\linfd{n}\to\linfd{n}$
(\texttt{def-stochastic}) with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prh-target}
  \opnorm{AM-E}\;\le\;2\sqrt{2\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
\emph{Transcription note.} The pair $(A,M)$ is the datum of
\texttt{def-positive-approximate-retract}, with retract defect
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ret}}=\opnorm{MA-I_k}$. Following the validated tree, $A$ is
presented by rows $a_i\in\Delta_k$ indexed by $i\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ and $M$ by rows
$\mu_s$ indexed by $s\in\{1,\dots,k\}$, so that the $s$-th row of $MA$ is the
probability vector $\sum_i\mu_s(i)\,a_i$.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textbf{Registry contract (verbatim).}
\contractquote{Positive-retract hardening (PRH): let k,n >= 1 and let A:l-infinity(k)->l-infinity(n) and M:l-infinity(n)->l-infinity(k) be positive unital maps (equivalently, have probability-vector rows); if ||MA-I_k||_{infinity->infinity} <= epsilon with 0 <= epsilon < 1/2, then there is a stochastic idempotent E:l-infinity(n)->l-infinity(n) with ||AM-E||_{infinity->infinity} <= 2*sqrt(2*epsilon).}

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
The tree splits on the defect. If $\varepsilon=0$ the hypothesis reads $MA=I_k$ exactly,
and then $E:=AM$ is already row-stochastic with $E^2=A(MA)M=AM=E$, so
\eqref{eq:prh-target} holds with error $0$. The substance is the case
$0<\varepsilon<\tfrac12$.

\emph{The scale and the cores.} Fix
\[
  \lambda=\sqrt{\varepsilon/2},\qquad
  C_s=\{\,i:\ a_{is}>1-\lambda\,\},\qquad
  \beta_s=\mu_s(C_s^{c}).
\]
The single quantitative input is a coordinate defect identity. The $s$-th row of $MA$ is
the probability vector $\sum_i\mu_s(i)a_i$, and for probability vectors the $\ell^1$
distance to the basis vector $e_s$ is governed by one coordinate:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prh-defect}
  \lone{(MA)_{s,*}-e_s}=2\bigl(1-(MA)_{ss}\bigr)=2\sum_i\mu_s(i)\,(1-a_{is})=:2D_s,
\end{equation}
so the norm hypothesis is exactly $D_s\le\varepsilon/2$ for every $s$. Two consequences
follow. First, the cores are pairwise disjoint: if $i\in C_s\cap C_t$ with $s\ne t$ then
$a_{is}+a_{it}>2(1-\lambda)>1$ (using $\lambda<\tfrac12$, which is where
$\varepsilon<\tfrac12$ enters), contradicting that $a_i$ is a probability vector. Second,
off the core one has $1-a_{is}\ge\lambda$, so dropping the on-core terms of the
nonnegative sum $D_s$ gives $\lambda\beta_s\le D_s\le\varepsilon/2$, whence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prh-beta}
  \beta_s\;\le\;\frac{\varepsilon}{2\lambda}\;=\;\lambda\;<\;\tfrac12 .
\end{equation}
The tree records a correction at this point: an earlier node asserted the strict
inequality $\beta_s<\lambda$, which the threshold $a_{is}\le1-\lambda$ off the core does
not give; the weak form \eqref{eq:prh-beta} is what holds and all that is used.

\emph{The exact construction.} Condition each decoder row on its core and replace each
encoder row inside a core by a basis vector:
\[
  \nu_s(i)=\frac{\mu_s(i)\,\mathbf1_{C_s}(i)}{1-\beta_s},
  \qquad
  \widehat A\ \text{has row}\ e_s\ \text{on}\ C_s\ \text{and row}\ a_i\ \text{off}\ \textstyle\bigcup_s C_s .
\]
Both are well defined: $\beta_s<1$ makes $\nu_s$ a probability vector, and disjointness of
the cores makes $\widehat A$ unambiguous. The construction is engineered so that the
retraction becomes \emph{exact}: the $s$-th row of $N\widehat A$ is
$\sum_{i\in C_s}\mu_s(i)e_s/(1-\beta_s)=e_s$, i.e.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prh-exact}
  N\widehat A=I_k,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad E:=\widehat AN\ \text{satisfies}\ E^2=\widehat A(N\widehat A)N=\widehat AN=E .
\end{equation}
As a product of positive unital maps, $E$ is row-stochastic, so it is a stochastic
idempotent in the sense of \texttt{def-stochastic}.

\emph{The two error terms.} Write $AM-E=A(M-N)+(A-\widehat A)N$ and bound the summands
separately.

For the conditioning term, $\nu_s$ is $\mu_s$ conditioned on $C_s$, so the two rows differ
by mass $\beta_s$ removed from $C_s^c$ and mass $\beta_s$ redistributed on $C_s$; hence
$\lone{\mu_s-\nu_s}=2\beta_s\le\varepsilon/\lambda$ by \eqref{eq:prh-beta} and
$\opnorm{M-N}\le\varepsilon/\lambda$. Because $A$ has probability rows it is an
$\infty\!\to\!\infty$ contraction --- concretely,
$\sum_j\lvert(AB)_{ij}\rvert\le\sum_s a_{is}\sum_j\lvert B_{sj}\rvert\le\opnorm{B}$ ---
so $\opnorm{A(M-N)}\le\varepsilon/\lambda$.

For the core-replacement term, the rows of $(A-\widehat A)N$ vanish outside
$\bigcup_sC_s$. For $i\in C_s$ the corresponding row is $\sum_t a_{it}\nu_t-\nu_s$, and
since every $\nu_t$ is a probability vector its $\ell^1$ norm is at most
$(1-a_{is})+\sum_{t\ne s}a_{it}=2(1-a_{is})<2\lambda$. Hence
$\opnorm{(A-\widehat A)N}\le2\lambda$.

\emph{Optimisation.} Adding the two bounds,
\[
  \opnorm{AM-E}\;\le\;\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}+2\lambda ,
\]
and the choice $\lambda=\sqrt{\varepsilon/2}$ balances the two terms at the common value
$\sqrt{2\varepsilon}$, giving \eqref{eq:prh-target}. The square-root rate is intrinsic to
this balance: conditioning costs $\varepsilon/\lambda$ and core replacement costs
$2\lambda$, and no choice of threshold does better than their geometric mean.

the finishing step of the live route: it installs the reduction of \texttt{op-classical}
to the \emph{existence} of a positive approximate retract for a given almost-idempotent
$Q$ --- stochastic $A,M$ with $\opnorm{AM-Q}=O(\defect)$ and $\opnorm{MA-I}=O(\defect)$ ---
after which \eqref{eq:prh-target} delivers the exact idempotent at rate $\sqrt{\defect}$.
Producing such a pair is what the COMP and H-CB tiers below are ultimately for, and that
production is \emph{not} established: \texttt{lem-prh} is a conditional finisher, and
\texttt{op-classical} remains open. Downstream registry consumers are
\texttt{lem-routef-prh-finish} and \texttt{lem-routef-k-ledger}, both non-rigorous.
