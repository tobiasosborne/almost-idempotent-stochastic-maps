\section{Positive-retract hardening}\label{sec:prh}

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{lem-prh}]\label{lem:prh}
Let $k,n\ge1$ and let
\[
  A:\linfd{k}\longrightarrow\linfd{n},
  \qquad
  M:\linfd{n}\longrightarrow\linfd{k}
\]
be positive unital maps --- equivalently, maps whose matrices have probability-vector
rows. If
\[
  \opnorm{MA-I_k}\;\le\;\varepsilon,\qquad 0\le\varepsilon<\tfrac12,
\]
then there is a stochastic idempotent $E:\linfd{n}\to\linfd{n}$
(\texttt{def-stochastic}) with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prh-target}
  \opnorm{AM-E}\;\le\;2\sqrt{2\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
\emph{Transcription note.} The pair $(A,M)$ is the datum of
\texttt{def-positive-approximate-retract}, with retract defect
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ret}}=\opnorm{MA-I_k}$. Following the validated tree, $A$ is
presented by rows $a_i\in\Delta_k$ indexed by $i\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ and $M$ by rows
$\mu_s$ indexed by $s\in\{1,\dots,k\}$, so that the $s$-th row of $MA$ is the
probability vector $\sum_i\mu_s(i)\,a_i$.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textbf{Registry contract (verbatim).}
\contractquote{Positive-retract hardening (PRH): let k,n >= 1 and let A:l-infinity(k)->l-infinity(n) and M:l-infinity(n)->l-infinity(k) be positive unital maps (equivalently, have probability-vector rows); if ||MA-I_k||_{infinity->infinity} <= epsilon with 0 <= epsilon < 1/2, then there is a stochastic idempotent E:l-infinity(n)->l-infinity(n) with ||AM-E||_{infinity->infinity} <= 2*sqrt(2*epsilon).}

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
The tree splits on the defect. If $\varepsilon=0$ the hypothesis reads $MA=I_k$ exactly,
and then $E:=AM$ is already row-stochastic with $E^2=A(MA)M=AM=E$, so
\eqref{eq:prh-target} holds with error $0$. The substance is the case
$0<\varepsilon<\tfrac12$.

\emph{The scale and the cores.} Fix
\[
  \lambda=\sqrt{\varepsilon/2},\qquad
  C_s=\{\,i:\ a_{is}>1-\lambda\,\},\qquad
  \beta_s=\mu_s(C_s^{c}).
\]
The single quantitative input is a coordinate defect identity. The $s$-th row of $MA$ is
the probability vector $\sum_i\mu_s(i)a_i$, and for probability vectors the $\ell^1$
distance to the basis vector $e_s$ is governed by one coordinate:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prh-defect}
  \lone{(MA)_{s,*}-e_s}=2\bigl(1-(MA)_{ss}\bigr)=2\sum_i\mu_s(i)\,(1-a_{is})=:2D_s,
\end{equation}
so the norm hypothesis is exactly $D_s\le\varepsilon/2$ for every $s$. Two consequences
follow. First, the cores are pairwise disjoint: if $i\in C_s\cap C_t$ with $s\ne t$ then
$a_{is}+a_{it}>2(1-\lambda)>1$ (using $\lambda<\tfrac12$, which is where
$\varepsilon<\tfrac12$ enters), contradicting that $a_i$ is a probability vector. Second,
off the core one has $1-a_{is}\ge\lambda$, so dropping the on-core terms of the
nonnegative sum $D_s$ gives $\lambda\beta_s\le D_s\le\varepsilon/2$, whence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prh-beta}
  \beta_s\;\le\;\frac{\varepsilon}{2\lambda}\;=\;\lambda\;<\;\tfrac12 .
\end{equation}
The tree records a correction at this point: an earlier node asserted the strict
inequality $\beta_s<\lambda$, which the threshold $a_{is}\le1-\lambda$ off the core does
not give; the weak form \eqref{eq:prh-beta} is what holds and all that is used.

\emph{The exact construction.} Condition each decoder row on its core and replace each
encoder row inside a core by a basis vector:
\[
  \nu_s(i)=\frac{\mu_s(i)\,\mathbf1_{C_s}(i)}{1-\beta_s},
  \qquad
  \widehat A\ \text{has row}\ e_s\ \text{on}\ C_s\ \text{and row}\ a_i\ \text{off}\ \textstyle\bigcup_s C_s .
\]
Both are well defined: $\beta_s<1$ makes $\nu_s$ a probability vector, and disjointness of
the cores makes $\widehat A$ unambiguous. The construction is engineered so that the
retraction becomes \emph{exact}: the $s$-th row of $N\widehat A$ is
$\sum_{i\in C_s}\mu_s(i)e_s/(1-\beta_s)=e_s$, i.e.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prh-exact}
  N\widehat A=I_k,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad E:=\widehat AN\ \text{satisfies}\ E^2=\widehat A(N\widehat A)N=\widehat AN=E .
\end{equation}
As a product of positive unital maps, $E$ is row-stochastic, so it is a stochastic
idempotent in the sense of \texttt{def-stochastic}.

\emph{The two error terms.} Write $AM-E=A(M-N)+(A-\widehat A)N$ and bound the summands
separately.

For the conditioning term, $\nu_s$ is $\mu_s$ conditioned on $C_s$, so the two rows differ
by mass $\beta_s$ removed from $C_s^c$ and mass $\beta_s$ redistributed on $C_s$; hence
$\lone{\mu_s-\nu_s}=2\beta_s\le\varepsilon/\lambda$ by \eqref{eq:prh-beta} and
$\opnorm{M-N}\le\varepsilon/\lambda$. Because $A$ has probability rows it is an
$\infty\!\to\!\infty$ contraction --- concretely,
$\sum_j\lvert(AB)_{ij}\rvert\le\sum_s a_{is}\sum_j\lvert B_{sj}\rvert\le\opnorm{B}$ ---
so $\opnorm{A(M-N)}\le\varepsilon/\lambda$.

For the core-replacement term, the rows of $(A-\widehat A)N$ vanish outside
$\bigcup_sC_s$. For $i\in C_s$ the corresponding row is $\sum_t a_{it}\nu_t-\nu_s$, and
since every $\nu_t$ is a probability vector its $\ell^1$ norm is at most
$(1-a_{is})+\sum_{t\ne s}a_{it}=2(1-a_{is})<2\lambda$. Hence
$\opnorm{(A-\widehat A)N}\le2\lambda$.

\emph{Optimisation.} Adding the two bounds,
\[
  \opnorm{AM-E}\;\le\;\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}+2\lambda ,
\]
and the choice $\lambda=\sqrt{\varepsilon/2}$ balances the two terms at the common value
$\sqrt{2\varepsilon}$, giving \eqref{eq:prh-target}. The square-root rate is intrinsic to
this balance: conditioning costs $\varepsilon/\lambda$ and core replacement costs
$2\lambda$, and no choice of threshold does better than their geometric mean.

\subsection*{The sharp square-root order}

The upper bound above has a matching T0 lower-bound family.  The four rows below separate
the finite arithmetic, the general Markov-chain fact, the PRH conclusion, and its direct
stochastic almost-idempotence corollary.

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{lem-prh-sharpness-family-arithmetic}]\label{lem:prh-sharpness-family-arithmetic}
For $0<\lambda<\tfrac12$, put
\[
A_\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\\1-\lambda&\lambda\\\lambda&1-\lambda\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M_\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}1-\lambda&0&\lambda&0\\0&1-\lambda&0&\lambda\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both matrices have nonnegative probability rows and hence represent positive unital maps
$A_\lambda:\linfd{2}\to\linfd{4}$ and $M_\lambda:\linfd{4}\to\linfd{2}$.  For every
$B\in\mathbb R^{r\times s}$, $r,s\ge1$, its induced norm is
$\opnorm{B}=\max_{1\le i\le r}\sum_{j=1}^{s}|B_{ij}|$.  Moreover,
\[
M_\lambda A_\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}1-\lambda^2&\lambda^2\\\lambda^2&1-\lambda^2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \varepsilon_\lambda:=\opnorm{M_\lambda A_\lambda-I_2}=2\lambda^2\longrightarrow0,
\]
and $P_\lambda:=A_\lambda M_\lambda$ is row-stochastic with rows
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1-\lambda&0&\lambda&0\\
0&1-\lambda&0&\lambda\\
(1-\lambda)^2&\lambda(1-\lambda)&\lambda(1-\lambda)&\lambda^2\\
\lambda(1-\lambda)&(1-\lambda)^2&\lambda^2&\lambda(1-\lambda)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \lone{\operatorname{row}_1(P_\lambda)-\operatorname{row}_3(P_\lambda)}=2\lambda.
\]
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textbf{Registry contract (verbatim).}
\contractquote{Witness arithmetic for PRH sharpness: for every real lambda with 0 < lambda < 1/2, let A_lambda in R^(4x2) have rows (1,0), (0,1), (1-lambda,lambda), (lambda,1-lambda), and let M_lambda in R^(2x4) have rows (1-lambda,0,lambda,0), (0,1-lambda,0,lambda); A_lambda and M_lambda have nonnegative entries and probability-vector rows and hence represent positive unital maps A_lambda:l-infinity(2)->l-infinity(4) and M_lambda:l-infinity(4)->l-infinity(2); for every pair of integers r,s >= 1 and every B in R^(r x s), the induced l-infinity operator norm is ||B||_{infinity->infinity}=max_{1<=i<=r} sum_{j=1}^s |B_ij|; M_lambda A_lambda has rows (1-lambda^2,lambda^2), (lambda^2,1-lambda^2), so epsilon_lambda:=||M_lambda A_lambda-I_2||_{infinity->infinity}=2*lambda^2 and epsilon_lambda tends to 0 as lambda tends to 0; and P_lambda:=A_lambda M_lambda is row-stochastic with rows (1-lambda,0,lambda,0), (0,1-lambda,0,lambda), ((1-lambda)^2,lambda*(1-lambda),lambda*(1-lambda),lambda^2), (lambda*(1-lambda),(1-lambda)^2,lambda^2,lambda*(1-lambda)), and ||row_1(P_lambda)-row_3(P_lambda)||_1=2*lambda.}

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
Positivity and the row sums are immediate from $0<\lambda<\tfrac12$.  For the norm
formula, the triangle inequality gives the maximum absolute row sum as an upper bound;
choosing signs against a row attaining that maximum gives the reverse inequality, including
the zero-row case.  Direct row-by-column multiplication gives the displayed $M_\lambda
A_\lambda$, whose two rows differ from those of $I_2$ by absolute row sum $2\lambda^2$.
The same multiplication gives all four displayed rows of $P_\lambda$; they are probability
rows, and subtracting its first and third rows gives exact $\ell^1$ norm $2\lambda$.  This is
the content of the validated 24-node export, including its repaired non-strict norm chain
and explicit per-row product derivations.

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{lem-prh-sharpness-row-coincidence}]\label{lem:prh-sharpness-row-coincidence}
Let $F=(f_{ab})$ be an $n\times n$ row-stochastic idempotent.  If
$f_{ii}>0$ and $f_{ij}>0$, then $\operatorname{row}_i(F)=\operatorname{row}_j(F)$.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textbf{Registry contract (verbatim).}
\contractquote{Row coincidence for stochastic idempotents: for every integer n >= 1, every n-by-n row-stochastic idempotent matrix F=(f_ab) over R, and all i,j in {1,...,n}, if f_ii>0 and f_ij>0 then row_i(F)=row_j(F).}

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
Set $p=\operatorname{row}_i(F)$ and $S=\{a:p_a>0\}$.  Idempotence makes $p$ stationary;
nonnegativity shows that $S$ is closed.  On the positive-entry graph of the restriction to
$S$, a source component with an outgoing edge would lose strictly positive stationary mass,
so no edge joins distinct strongly connected components.  Since $f_{ia}=p_a>0$ for every
$a\in S$, the graph is strongly connected.  The row $q=\operatorname{row}_j(F)$ is supported
on $S$ and stationary there.  Finally, with $c=\min_a q_a/p_a$, the nonnegative stationary
row $q-cp$ has a zero coordinate; strong connectivity forces every nonzero nonnegative
stationary row to be strictly positive, so $c=1$ and $q=p$.  This follows the validated
19-node export; its cross-sibling repairs make the closure, stationarity, and uniqueness
steps dependency-explicit.

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{lem-prh-sharpness}]\label{lem:prh-sharpness}
For every $0<\lambda<\tfrac12$ there are positive unital maps
$A:\linfd{2}\to\linfd{4}$ and $M:\linfd{4}\to\linfd{2}$ with
$\varepsilon_\lambda=\opnorm{MA-I_2}=2\lambda^2\to0$ such that every stochastic
idempotent $F$ on $\linfd{4}$ satisfies
\[
\opnorm{AM-F}\ge\lambda=\sqrt{\varepsilon_\lambda/2}.
\]
Thus the $\sqrt\varepsilon$ order in PRH is intrinsically sharp.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textbf{Registry contract (verbatim).}
\contractquote{PRH square-root sharpness: for every 0 < lambda < 1/2 there are positive unital maps A:l-infinity(2)->l-infinity(4) and M:l-infinity(4)->l-infinity(2) with epsilon_lambda=||MA-I_2||_{infinity->infinity}=2*lambda^2 tending to 0 such that every stochastic idempotent F on l-infinity(4) satisfies ||AM-F||_{infinity->infinity} >= lambda=sqrt(epsilon_lambda/2); hence the sqrt(epsilon) order in PRH is intrinsically sharp.}

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
Use $A_\lambda,M_\lambda,P_\lambda$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:prh-sharpness-family-arithmetic}.
If a stochastic idempotent $F$ had $\opnorm{P_\lambda-F}<\lambda$, the max-row norm
formula would give $f_{11}>1-2\lambda>0$ and $f_{13}>0$.  The row-coincidence lemma would
then make rows $1$ and $3$ of $F$ equal, while the triangle inequality would put the two
corresponding rows of $P_\lambda$ at distance strictly less than $2\lambda$, contradicting
their exact distance.  Hence every $F$ is at least $\lambda$ away; since
$\varepsilon_\lambda=2\lambda^2$, this is $\sqrt{\varepsilon_\lambda/2}$.  The factored
main tree validated first-pass with 12 nodes and zero challenges after the two earlier
monolithic attempts ballooned.

\begin{corollary}[\texttt{cor-classical-sharpness}]\label{cor:classical-sharpness}
For $0<\lambda<\tfrac12$, choose the preceding $A_\lambda,M_\lambda$ and put
$\eta_\lambda=2\lambda^2$, $Q_\lambda=A_\lambda M_\lambda$.  Then $Q_\lambda$ is
row-stochastic,
\[
\opnorm{Q_\lambda^2-Q_\lambda}\le\eta_\lambda,
\qquad
\opnorm{Q_\lambda-F}\ge\lambda=\sqrt{\eta_\lambda/2}
\]
for every stochastic idempotent $F$.  Consequently, for every $C>0$, $\eta_0>0$, and
$\beta>\tfrac12$, some $0<\eta<\min\{\eta_0,\tfrac14\}$ and row-stochastic $Q$ satisfy
$\opnorm{Q^2-Q}\le\eta$ while every stochastic idempotent $E$ satisfies
$\opnorm{Q-E}>C\eta^\beta$.  Thus no uniform exponent $\beta>\tfrac12$ can replace
$\tfrac12$ in \texttt{op-classical}.
\end{corollary}

\noindent\textbf{Registry contract (verbatim).}
\contractquote{Classical square-root sharpness: for every 0 < lambda < 1/2, choose positive unital maps A_lambda:l-infinity(2)->l-infinity(4) and M_lambda:l-infinity(4)->l-infinity(2) supplied by lem-prh-sharpness, and put eta_lambda=2*lambda^2 and Q_lambda=A_lambda M_lambda; then Q_lambda is row-stochastic, ||Q_lambda^2-Q_lambda||_{infinity->infinity} <= eta_lambda, and every stochastic idempotent F on l-infinity(4) satisfies ||Q_lambda-F||_{infinity->infinity} >= lambda=sqrt(eta_lambda/2). Consequently, for every C>0, eta_0>0, and beta>1/2 there exist 0<eta<min{eta_0,1/4} and a row-stochastic Q on l-infinity(4) with ||Q^2-Q||_{infinity->infinity} <= eta such that every stochastic idempotent E satisfies ||Q-E||_{infinity->infinity} > C*eta^beta; equivalently, no uniform exponent beta>1/2 can replace 1/2 in op-classical.}

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
Products of probability-row matrices are row-stochastic and have operator norm one.  With
$Q_\lambda=A_\lambda M_\lambda$ defined at the root,
\[
Q_\lambda^2-Q_\lambda=A_\lambda(M_\lambda A_\lambda-I_2)M_\lambda,
\]
so submultiplicativity gives the stated weak defect bound; the preceding lemma gives the
distance lower bound.  Given $C,\eta_0,\beta$, choose
\[
0<\lambda<\min\left\{\frac1{2\sqrt2},\sqrt{\frac{\eta_0}{2}},
(C2^\beta)^{-1/(2\beta-1)}\right\}.
\]
Then $\eta=2\lambda^2$ has the required range and
$C\eta^\beta=C2^\beta\lambda^{2\beta}<\lambda\le\opnorm{Q_\lambda-E}$ for every $E$.
This is the literal dimension-four counterexample to any proposed uniform
$(C,\eta_0,\beta)$ estimate.  Run 1 ballooned at 26 live nodes; under the user-ratified,
hostile-audited remedy-(b) linear-node design, the af tree validated first-pass with all
five nodes clean, zero challenges, and only the validated PRH sharpness external.
