\section{Quantitative approximate group laws on the unitaries}\label{sec:stage1-group-laws}

The unitary set $\Unit$ of Definition~\ref{def:approximate-unitary-space} is not a group:
$\bdot$ is only approximately associative and $U\dg$ is only approximately an inverse.
The three results below aim to turn it into an approximate group by \emph{projecting} the
naive product and adjoint back to $\Unit$ through the polar inverse $u_\delta$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-polar-retraction}. The first two are \texttt{af}-validated; the third
--- the compound parent --- is \textbf{retired in place} (below). Throughout, all three
carry the same pair of guards
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s1gl-guards}
  C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\ecs_r+\delta)\le\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}},
  \qquad
  C_{\mathrm{grp}}\ecs_r<\delta-C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\ecs_r\delta+\delta^{2}),
\end{equation}
with universal $C_{\mathrm{grp}},C_{\mathrm{pol}}\ge1$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}\in(0,\tfrac12]$.

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{lem-stage1-group-domain-membership}]\label{lem:stage1-group-domain-membership}
There exist universal $C_{\mathrm{grp}},C_{\mathrm{pol}}\ge1$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}\in(0,\tfrac12]$ such that for every finite-dimensional exact-unit
$\ecs_r$-$C^*$-algebra and every $\delta>0$ satisfying \eqref{eq:s1gl-guards}, the inverse
$u_\delta:S_\delta\to\Unit$ of the polar map is defined at $U\bdot V$ and at $U\dg$ for every
$U,V\in\Unit$; moreover $U\bdot V$ and $U\dg$ each have a right inverse.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textbf{Registry contract (verbatim).}
\contractquote{Group-input polar-domain membership: there exist universal C_grp, C_pol >= 1, kappa_pol in (0, 1/2] such that for every finite-dimensional exact-unit epsilon_r-C*-algebra and delta > 0 satisfying C_pol*(epsilon_r + delta) <= kappa_pol and C_grp*epsilon_r < delta - C_pol*(epsilon_r*delta + delta^2), the inverse u_delta:S_delta -> calU of the polar map is defined at U bold-dot V and U^dagger for every U, V in calU; moreover, U bold-dot V and U^dagger each have a right inverse.}

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
Take $C_{\mathrm{pol}},\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-polar-retraction}
and $C_{\mathrm{grp}}=4$; abbreviate $e=\ecs_r$ and
$t=\delta-C_{\mathrm{pol}}(e\delta+\delta^{2})$. By the inner inclusion
\eqref{eq:s1pr-sandwich} it suffices to place $U\bdot V$ and $U\dg$ in $\Unito{t}$,
right-inverse clause included.

\emph{In-scope smallness.} Everything below cites one arithmetic node, derived from
\eqref{eq:s1gl-guards} alone: $C_{\mathrm{pol}}\ge1$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}\le\tfrac12$
give $s:=e+\delta\le\tfrac12$ and $\delta\le\tfrac12$, while the second guard with
$C_{\mathrm{grp}}=4$ gives $4e<t$, hence $t>0$ and $e<t\le\delta(1-s)$. Since
$(s-e)(1-s)$ is increasing in $s$ on $[e,\tfrac12]$, this forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s1gl-small}
  e<\tfrac16 .
\end{equation}
No threshold beyond \eqref{eq:s1gl-small} is available, and none is used.

\emph{Multiplier control.} For $W\in\Unit$, $W\dg\bdot W=J$ and the $C^*$-lower bound give
$(1-e)\nrm{W}^{2}\le1$, while the associator axiom gives
$\nrm{\Lmul{W\dg}\Lmul{W}-I}\le e\nrm{W}^{2}\le e/(1-e)<\tfrac15$ by \eqref{eq:s1gl-small}.
Hence $\Lmul{W}$ is injective, therefore bijective in finite dimension, with
$\nrm{\Lmul{W\dg}}\le\tfrac75$ and $\nrm{\Lmul{W}^{-1}}\le\tfrac74$.

\emph{The product.} Put $X=U\bdot V$, so $X\dg=V\dg\bdot U\dg$ by the star rule. Inserting
$((V\dg\bdot U\dg)\bdot U)\bdot V$ and $(V\dg\bdot(U\dg\bdot U))\bdot V$ between
$X\dg\bdot X$ and $J$ costs two associators and lands on
$(V\dg\bdot J)\bdot V=V\dg\bdot V=J$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s1gl-proddefect}
  \nrm{X\dg\bdot X-J}\;\le\;\frac{2e(1+e)}{(1-e)^{2}}\;\le\;4e\;<\;t\;<\;2t .
\end{equation}
Approximate associativity gives $\nrm{\Lmul{X}-\Lmul{U}\Lmul{V}}\le e\nrm{U}\nrm{V}<\tfrac15$,
while $\nrm{(\Lmul{U}\Lmul{V})^{-1}}\le\tfrac{49}{16}$; the Neumann perturbation is
$<\tfrac{49}{80}<1$, so $\Lmul{X}$ is invertible and $\Lmul{X}^{-1}J$ is a right inverse
of $X$.

\emph{The adjoint.} $U\dg\bdot U=J$ says $U$ is a right inverse of $U\dg$. If $R$ is the
right inverse of $U$ supplied by Definition~\ref{def:approximate-unitary-space} then
$R=\Lmul{U}^{-1}J$ and $\nrm{R}\le\tfrac74$; one associator gives
$\nrm{U\dg-R}\le e\nrm{U}^{2}\nrm{R}$ and hence
$\nrm{U\bdot U\dg-J}\le(1+e)e\nrm{U}^{3}\nrm{R}\le\tfrac{441}{125}e<4e<t<2t$. So both inputs
lie in $\Unito{t}\subseteq S_\delta$.

\emph{Endpoint discipline.} Every estimate above is non-strict and remains meaningful at
$e=0$; the single strict passage is $C_{\mathrm{grp}}e<t$, followed by $t<2t$ because
$t>0$. That is exactly what membership in the strict set $\Unito{t}$ needs.

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{lem-stage1-group-closeness}]\label{lem:stage1-group-closeness}
There exist universal $C_{\mathrm{grp}},C_{\mathrm{pol}}\ge1$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}\in(0,\tfrac12]$ such that for every finite-dimensional exact-unit
$\ecs_r$-$C^*$-algebra and every $\delta>0$ satisfying \eqref{eq:s1gl-guards}, the inverse
$u_\delta:S_\delta\to\Unit$ of the polar map satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s1gl-close}
  \nrm{u_\delta(U\bdot V)-U\bdot V}\le C_{\mathrm{grp}}\ecs_r,
  \qquad
  \nrm{u_\delta(U\dg)-U\dg}\le C_{\mathrm{grp}}\ecs_r
  \qquad(U,V\in\Unit).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textbf{Registry contract (verbatim).}
\contractquote{Group-input polar closeness: there exist universal C_grp, C_pol >= 1, kappa_pol in (0, 1/2] such that for every finite-dimensional exact-unit epsilon_r-C*-algebra and delta > 0 satisfying C_pol*(epsilon_r + delta) <= kappa_pol and C_grp*epsilon_r < delta - C_pol*(epsilon_r*delta + delta^2), the inverse u_delta:S_delta -> calU of the polar map satisfies ||u_delta(U bold-dot V) - U bold-dot V|| <= C_grp*epsilon_r and ||u_delta(U^dagger) - U^dagger|| <= C_grp*epsilon_r for every U, V in calU.}

\paragraph{Proof (prose account of the af-validated tree).}
Take $\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}\le\tfrac1{16}$ and $C_{\mathrm{grp}}=5$, and write
$\rho=\delta-C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\ecs_r\delta+\delta^{2})$, so $\ecs_r+\delta\le\tfrac1{16}$ and
$\rho>5\ecs_r\ge0$. This row deliberately does \emph{not} import
Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-group-domain-membership}: the displayed expressions assert that both
inputs lie in the domain of $u_\delta$, and the tree re-derives that typing internally ---
$\nrm{(U\bdot V)\dg\bdot(U\bdot V)-J}\le3\ecs_r$ with a right inverse obtained from
injectivity of $\Lmul{U\bdot V}$ in finite dimension, and
$\nrm{U\bdot U\dg-J}\le2\ecs_r$ with the exact right inverse $U$ --- so that
$U\bdot V,U\dg\in\Unito{\rho}\subseteq S_\delta$. The duplication is the price of removing
every edge between the two children.

The estimate itself is one calculation, stated for any $X\in S_\delta$ with
$\nrm{X\dg\bdot X-J}\le3\ecs_r$. Write $u=u_\delta(X)$, $h=h_\delta(X)$ and $a=h-J$, so
$X=u\bdot h$, $u\in\Unit$, $h\in\Herm$ and $\nrm{a}<\delta$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-polar-retraction}. Two associator comparisons of
$(h\bdot u\dg)\bdot(u\bdot h)$ against $h\bdot h$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s1gl-square}
  \nrm{X\dg\bdot X-h\bdot h}\;\le\;2\ecs_r(1+\ecs_r)\nrm{h}^{2}\nrm{u}^{2}\;\le\;3\ecs_r .
\end{equation}
Exact unitality and bilinearity give $h\bdot h-J=2a+a\bdot a$, so
$2\nrm{a}\le6\ecs_r+(1+\ecs_r)\nrm{a}^{2}$; since $\nrm{a}<\delta\le\tfrac1{16}$ the
quadratic term is at most $\tfrac{17}{256}\nrm{a}$ and absorbs, leaving
$\nrm{a}\le\tfrac{1536}{495}\ecs_r\le4\ecs_r$. Finally exact right unitality gives
$\nrm{u_\delta(X)-X}=\nrm{u\bdot(J-h)}\le(1+\ecs_r)\nrm{u}\nrm{a}\le5\ecs_r$, which is
\eqref{eq:s1gl-close}.

\begin{conjecture}[\texttt{lem-stage1-approximate-group-laws} --- proof retracted 2026-07-28]\label{lem:stage1-approximate-group-laws}
There exist universal $C_{\mathrm{grp}},C_{\mathrm{pol}}\ge1$ and

$u_\delta$ of the polar map defines $C^{1}$ maps
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s1gl-musigma}
  \mu(U,V)=u_\delta(U\bdot V),\qquad \sigma(U)=u_\delta(U\dg)

\begin{gather}

\paragraph{Historical record: the RETRACTED proof attempt (not a proof; defect marked).}
\emph{Witness synchronisation --- THE DEFECTIVE STEP.} If $(G_d,P_d,k_d)$, $(G_c,P_c,k_c)$
and $(P_r,k_r)$ are witnesses for the two lemmas above and for
Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-polar-retraction}, put $C_{\mathrm{pol}}=\max(P_d,P_c,P_r)$,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{pol}}=\min(k_d,k_c,k_r,\tfrac1{16})$ and
$C_{\mathrm{grp}}=\max(G_d,8G_c,8)$. Both guards are monotone in these witnesses, so
\eqref{eq:s1gl-guards} implies each upstream guard, and $\ecs_r\le\tfrac1{16}$. The tree
then claimed: every polar datum in play uses the \emph{same} map $\Pi_\delta$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-polar-coherence-naturality} identifies the inverse components.
\emph{This is the sweep-adjudicated defect}: the children's contracts export only an
anaphoric first component --- no typed companion, no displayed source map, no preimage
identity --- so coherence's two-typed-data antecedent is unavailable and no ``one common
$u_\delta$'' is obtained.

\emph{Regularity and basepoints.} $U\bdot V$ and $U\dg$ lie in the open set $S_\delta$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-group-domain-membership}, and $u_\delta$ is $C^{1}$ there; the product
is bilinear and $\dagger$ is real-linear, hence both are $C^{1}$ in finite dimension, so
\eqref{eq:s1gl-musigma} defines $C^{1}$ maps on all of $\Unit$. Since $J\in\Unit$,
$J\bdot U=U\bdot J=U$, $J\dg=J$ and $u_\delta$ fixes $\Unit$ pointwise
(\eqref{eq:s1pr-inverse}), the three basepoint identities are exact. Estimate
\eqref{eq:s1gl-parent-close} is Lemma~\ref{lem:stage1-group-closeness} together with
$C_{\mathrm{grp}}\ge8G_c$.

\emph{The three defects.} All of $\Unit$ obeys $\nrm{T}\le\sqrt{16/15}<\tfrac43$, by the
$C^*$-lower bound applied to $T\dg\bdot T=J$. For \eqref{eq:s1gl-assoc}, insert
$A\bdot W$, $(U\bdot V)\bdot W$, $U\bdot(V\bdot W)$ and $U\bdot B$ between the two iterated
products, where $A=\mu(U,V)$ and $B=\mu(V,W)$: the two outer differences are each
$\le G_c\ecs_r$, each replacement difference is $\le\tfrac{17}{12}G_c\ecs_r$ by the
product-norm axiom, and the sole reassociation costs $\le\tfrac{64}{27}\ecs_r$; the total is
$\le\tfrac{389}{54}G_c\ecs_r\le8G_c\ecs_r$. For the first bound of
\eqref{eq:s1gl-inverse}, closeness at $(\sigma(U),U)$ plus
$\nrm{\sigma(U)\bdot U-U\dg\bdot U}\le\tfrac{17}{12}G_c\ecs_r$ and $U\dg\bdot U=J$ give
$\le3G_c\ecs_r$. For the second, the same two steps land on $U\bdot U\dg$, and a local
estimate --- $R=\Lmul{U}^{-1}J$ with $\nrm{R}\le\tfrac32$, one associator for
$\nrm{R-U\dg}$, then $U\bdot U\dg-J=U\bdot(U\dg-R)$ --- gives
$\nrm{U\bdot U\dg-J}\le\tfrac{34}{9}\ecs_r\le4\ecs_r$, for a total
$\le\tfrac{77}{12}G_c\ecs_r\le8G_c\ecs_r$. All bounds are non-strict and hold at
$\ecs_r=0$.
